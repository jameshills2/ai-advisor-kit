# Copperleaf Landscape Co. - Business Brief

## The business
Landscape design, installation and maintenance for homeowners in Alder Falls and nearby suburbs. Founded nine years ago by Rosa Delacroix, a horticulturist who started with mowing and grew into design-build. Rosa's one-liner: "We design gardens people actually keep alive, and then we help them keep them alive."

## Money (last 12 months, approximate)
| Source | Revenue | Share | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design and installation projects | $430,000 | 55% | Steady; booked 6 to 10 weeks out in spring |
| Weekly and biweekly maintenance | $275,000 | 35% | Growing slowly; most profitable per hour |
| Fall and spring cleanups | $78,000 | 10% | Flat |
| **Total** | **~$783,000** | | |

- Net margin: about 11% (Rosa's estimate; UNKNOWN by service line).
- Cash flow is seasonal: December to February revenue is near zero, while payroll for the five full-time staff continues.

## Customers
- Homeowners, mostly 40 to 70, in established neighborhoods with mature yards.
- About 70% of new work comes from referrals and yard signs. No paid advertising.
- Why they choose Copperleaf: Rosa's plant knowledge, plantings that survive, and a crew that shows up when promised.

## Resources and constraints
- Team: Rosa, a crew lead (Marcus), three full-time crew, two seasonal (April to October). Office help: Rosa's part-time bookkeeper.
- Equipment: two trucks, one trailer, one skid steer. No snow equipment.
- Rosa works 60+ hours a week in season and wants that to come down.
- Will not do: chemical lawn programs, commercial property maintenance, anything that requires Rosa to stop designing.
- Budget she can risk on a new idea: about $15,000 without borrowing.

## Goals
- **12 months:** steady winter cash flow; Rosa down to 50 hours a week in season.
- **3 years:** $1M revenue, a second crew lead so the business runs without Rosa on site every day.
- Why: "I want to design, not chase invoices in January."

## Track record
- **Retail plant stand (year 4):** ran it two summers. Lost money; took Rosa off job sites. Lesson she named: "Anything that needs me standing in one place kills the business."
- **Facebook ads (year 6):** $3,000 spent, two small jobs. Referral customers were better clients.
- **Irrigation service (year 7):** stalled because no one on the crew was certified.

## Exposure
- Work on customer property: damage, injuries, underground utilities.
- Seasonal staff and payroll; worker classification.
- Customer contracts are a one-page estimate plus a signature. No formal terms.

## How Rosa decides
"I say yes too fast when a customer asks for something. Then I'm stuck doing it." Tends to over-commit in spring.

## How Rosa wants to be challenged
Direct, no sugarcoating, but always offer the better version. Never suggest she stop designing. Don't recommend debt as the first answer.
