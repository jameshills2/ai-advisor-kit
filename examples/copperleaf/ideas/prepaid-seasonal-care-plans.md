## Verdict and why
**BUILD IT**, as a small test. It goes straight at the 12-month goal (winter cash flow), sells to customers who already trust Copperleaf, and needs no new equipment or skills.

## Concept
Maintenance customers pay for the full season (spring cleanup, biweekly maintenance April to October, fall cleanup) in advance, in November or December, at a 5% discount. Optionally split into three payments: December, February, April.

## What it builds on
- 35% of revenue is already maintenance, and it's the most profitable per hour.
- The idea came from a customer asking to prepay.
- Referral-driven customers who already trust the crew.

## The value
1. Cash arrives in December to February, when revenue is currently near zero.
2. The maintenance schedule is locked before spring, so Rosa isn't selling during the busiest weeks.
3. Customers who prepay are less likely to switch providers mid-season.

## Honest risks and tradeoffs
- **Discount cost:** 5% of maintenance revenue if everyone joined. Mitigation: offer it only to existing customers in year one.
- **Refunds:** what happens if a customer moves or is unhappy in June? Needs written terms (legal-risk).
- **Spending the cash too early:** prepaid money is owed in work. Mitigation: hold it in a separate account and release it monthly (finance).
- **Capacity:** locking in customers doesn't help if the crew is already full. Cap the number of plans.

## Panel notes
- **Finance (support):** if 40% of maintenance customers join, about $105,000 arrives before spring. Keep it in a separate account.
- **Legal-risk (caution):** needs written terms covering cancellation, refunds, weather delays and a pro-rated refund schedule. Questions prepared for the attorney.
- **Operations-tech (support):** no new work, and it makes spring scheduling easier. Needs a simple tracker of which visits have been delivered.
- **Customer-voice (support):** "I'd do it for the discount and the peace of mind, as long as I can get my money back if I move."

## Smallest test
Offer the plan to the 30 longest-standing maintenance customers in November, with attorney-reviewed terms. Success = 12 or more signups.

## What would change the verdict
Fewer than 6 signups (demand isn't there), or the attorney flags prepayment rules that make it impractical.
