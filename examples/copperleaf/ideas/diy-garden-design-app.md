## Verdict and why
**NOT A GOOD IDEA** for Copperleaf. It's a different business: software, sold to people who don't want to hire a landscaper, competing with funded companies. It uses none of Copperleaf's advantages and would pull Rosa away from design, her stated priority.

## Concept
A phone app where homeowners photograph their yard and get plant recommendations and a simple design based on Rosa's knowledge of what survives locally.

## What it builds on
Rosa's plant knowledge. Nothing else: no software skills, no app customers, no distribution.

## The value
1. Possible income that isn't tied to crew hours.
2. Brand visibility.

## Honest risks and tradeoffs
- **Wrong customer:** app users are, by definition, people who won't hire Copperleaf.
- **Cost:** building and maintaining an app costs far more than the $15,000 risk budget.
- **Competition:** funded competitors exist, which is where the idea came from.
- **Pattern:** like the plant stand, it pulls Rosa away from the work that makes money.

## Panel notes
- **Finance (oppose):** no realistic path to break-even within the risk budget.
- **Legal-risk (caution):** plant advice that kills a customer's yard creates complaints with no contract terms behind them.
- **Operations-tech (oppose):** nobody in the business can build or maintain software.
- **Customer-voice (oppose):** "I hire Copperleaf so I don't have to do this myself."

## The lesson
"Someone else raised money for it" is a reason to read about an idea, not to build it. The real asset here is Rosa's local plant knowledge. A cheaper way to use it: a printed "plants that survive Alder Falls" guide handed to every design client, which supports referrals instead of competing with them.

## Smallest test
None recommended for the app. The plant guide could be tested by handing it to the next 10 design clients.

## What would change the verdict
A software company offering to license Rosa's expertise, with no build cost or time commitment from Copperleaf.
