## Verdict and why
**NOT NOW.** The demand signal is real, and the 3-year goal points this way. But a second location needs a second crew lead, and Copperleaf doesn't have one. Today it would mean Rosa driving between two towns, adding hours instead of cutting them.

## Concept
A small yard and second crew based in Brookhaven, serving the referrals coming from there.

## What it builds on
- Referrals already arriving from Brookhaven.
- A proven service model and reputation.

## The value
1. A bigger market to grow into toward $1M.
2. Shorter drive times for Brookhaven jobs.
3. Room for a second crew lead to grow into a manager.

## Honest risks and tradeoffs
- **No second leader:** without one, Rosa is the manager of both locations.
- **Lease and equipment:** fixed costs before the revenue exists.
- **Quality:** referrals depend on the crew showing up when promised. A thin second crew puts the reputation at risk in both towns.

## Panel notes
- **Finance (caution):** a lease plus a third truck is roughly a year of risk budget. It only works if the care plans fix the winter cash gap first.
- **Legal-risk (caution):** lease terms and an exit clause need attorney review.
- **Operations-tech (oppose for now):** the bottleneck is people, not demand.
- **Customer-voice (caution):** "I'd use them in Brookhaven if it was the same crew quality. If not, I'd tell my neighbors."

## Smallest test
Serve Brookhaven from Alder Falls for one season with a cap of 10 clients, scheduled on two set days per week. That measures real demand without a lease.

## What would change the verdict
A second crew lead (possibly Marcus) running a crew independently for a full season, plus winter cash flow fixed by the care plans.
