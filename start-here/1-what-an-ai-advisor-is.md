# 1. What An AI Advisor Is (And Isn't)

## The problem it solves

AI chat tools are trained to be helpful. Ask one about your new idea and it will usually find a way to help you do it. That feels great, and it's the problem.

- **It only knows what's in the chat.** It doesn't know your revenue, your goals, the idea that failed last year or how many hours you really have.
- **It wants to answer fast.** It answers the question you asked, even when the bigger question is whether you should do this at all.
- **It agrees.** When you have a bad idea, agreement makes it feel like a good one. You don't see the roadblocks, so you can't plan for them, and you never get the chance to decide "not now."

Friends and family have a similar problem. They want you to feel good.

## What an advisor does differently

An AI advisor is the same AI, set up with two things:

1. **Your business context.** A written brief of what you sell, who buys, what pays the bills, your constraints and your goals. The advisor weighs every idea against it.
2. **A job description that rewards honesty.** Its goal is your business succeeding, and the way it shows that is by asking the hard questions first: who pays, what has to be true, what could go wrong, what's the smallest test.

It's not a yes-man and it's not a critic who kills everything. When it says no, it offers the version that could work.

## What it isn't

- **It isn't the decision maker.** You are. The point is better decisions that are yours, not "let the AI do it."
- **It isn't a lawyer, accountant or doctor.** Specialist roles can spot issues and prepare your questions. Take those questions to a qualified professional.
- **It isn't right all the time.** It only knows what you've told it. When it's missing context, correct it. That back and forth is where the best thinking happens.

## The other half: an Idea Inbox

Ideas show up at bad times: mid-meeting, mid-project, at 11pm. The advisor can **park** an idea instead of derailing you: it writes a one-page record of the idea, the reasoning, the risks and a verdict, then files it.

Over time those records become a library of your thinking. Most ideas will be "not now." That's fine. The library is something you browse whenever you like, in any order, and the advisor can check new ideas against old ones to find connections you'd miss.
